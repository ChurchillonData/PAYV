Paying digitally should feel as natural as handing over cash. One tap, one confirmation, done. No app to open, no number to type, no menu to navigate.

The problem

Face to face, digital money still takes five steps.

Mobile money is brilliant for sending money home. At a trotro door, a market stall or a canteen queue, the steps add up on every small payment, all day.

With PAYV Tap. Done. One gesture. No phone required.

Merchants have a second problem.

Most payment terminals are built for supermarkets: big screens, many features, high cost. A trader needs four things: key in an amount, take a tap, see the result, check the balance. PAYV is built around exactly that.

One network. Four parts.

Physical where the payment happens. Digital everywhere else.

PAYV Card

Contactless and secure. Pay without opening an app. Works for people without smartphones, and can be blocked and replaced if lost.

PAYV Terminal

Enter an amount, press the check, take the tap, see the result. Balance on its own key. No smartphone needed at the till.

PAYV App

The control centre. Balance, top-up from mobile money, withdrawal to MoMo or a bank, full history and card controls.

PAYV Network

The authorisation and ledger behind every tap, connected to licensed banks and mobile-money partners.

No cash.
By design.

PAYV moves and records digital money through licensed partners. It never touches a note or a coin.

PAYV does

  • Top up from mobile money
  • Pay merchants with a card tap
  • Withdraw to MoMo or a bank account
  • Show balances and full history
  • Connect to licensed banks and MoMo providers

PAYV doesn't

  • Collect cash for PAYV balances
  • Run counters or cash tills
  • Operate a cash agent network
  • Send staff to move money
  • Sell terminals that take or give cash

Who does what

Licensed partner

Holds and safeguards customer funds, issues the e-money, settles payments and runs required checks.

PAYV

The brand, cards, terminals, software, ledger operations and merchant experience, within its permitted role.

Regulator

A supervised pilot through the Bank of Ghana regulatory sandbox before full launch.

Exact structure to be confirmed with the partner and Ghanaian payments counsel before any live funds move.

Why now

Cheaper digital money

Ghana repealed the 1% e-levy on electronic transfers in April 2025. The money is already digital; the moment of payment isn't.

A path to pilot

The Bank of Ghana regulatory sandbox lets new payment models test under supervision, alongside licensed partners.

The tap is arriving

Transport tap cards and bank tap-to-pay launches are teaching the gesture. The gap is one network built for the everyday till.
